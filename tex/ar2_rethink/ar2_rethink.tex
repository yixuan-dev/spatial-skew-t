\title{
  AR(2) in a Spatial Skew-$t$ Model:\\
  Marginal Invariance, the Level-Autocovariance Ceiling,\\
  Predictive Scoring, and the Weak Identifiability of $\phi$
}
\author{Technical Note --- \texttt{ar2\_rethink}}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

% ====================================================================
\begin{abstract}
We document a connected sequence of derivations about the AR(2)
temporal prior placed on the latent processes of a spatial skew-$t$
model. The AR(2) construction normalises each latent so that its
stationary marginal variance equals one; we prove this makes the
time-$t$ marginal of every latent invariant to the autoregressive
coefficients $\phi$. The Brier score under a spatial-only holdout
integrates the predictive kernel against precisely this marginal, so
the temporal coupling marginalises out: the AR(2) methods fail to
dominate their i.i.d.-in-time siblings. We then sharpen the analysis in
five directions. First, even a \emph{perfectly specified} $\phi$
(frozen at the truth, zero estimation cost) cannot dominate, because
the surviving posterior route delivers only an $O(q_t^{-2})$ variance
reduction confined to non-spatial-noise components. Second --- the
central new result --- we decompose the \emph{level} autocovariance of
the observed process exactly: $\Cov(Y_t,Y_{t+h})=\lambda^2\gamma_z(h)$,
so AR(2) reaches the level only through the skew channel $\lambda z$;
the AR(2) on the scale $\tau$ is pure stochastic volatility, invisible
to every score that is a functional of the predictive law of the level.
Because the simulation truth sets $\phi_z=(0,0)$, the level of $Y$ is
\emph{exactly white in time}, and no level-based score can reward the
AR(2) prior for any holdout design --- a temporal signal-to-noise ratio
$\SNR$ caps the achievable gain, with a closed-form CRPS ceiling
$1-\sqrt{1-R(h)}$. Third, allowing $\phi$ to mix is more valuable for
the convergence of the \emph{other} parameters than pinning $\phi$
accurately. Fourth, a misspecified $\phi$ still reduces posterior
variance but pays in bias; AR(2) collapses to i.i.d.\ only as
$\phi\to0$. Fifth, the observed collapse $\hat\phi\to0$ is an
errors-in-variables attenuation. We close with a scoring methodology ---
block-time holdout, per-horizon multivariate scores, marginal
standardisation by the PIT, and a detectability calculus --- under which
the temporal structure that does exist becomes testable.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em, itemsep=2pt]
  \item The simulation study in \path{code/analysis/simstudy/} compares
        eight prediction methods for spatial extremes; methods 7 and 8
        place an AR(2) prior on every latent temporal process.
  \item Tabulated Brier scores for settings 9 and 10 show the AR(2)
        methods do \emph{not} systematically beat their i.i.d.\ siblings;
        in setting 10 the $K=5$ AR(2) variant is worse than the $K=5$
        no-AR baseline at every quantile.
  \item This note explains the discrepancy and the follow-on questions
        it raised. The conclusions concern the interaction of the AR(2)
        standardisation, the holdout geometry, the scoring rule, the
        MCMC sampler, and the identifiability of $\phi$ --- not bugs in
        \verb|simulate_ar2_standard()|.
  \item The decisive structural fact, isolated in
        Section~\ref{sec:level-autocov}, is that AR(2) enters the
        \emph{level} of the observed field through a single channel.
        Two of the three AR(2) processes the model fits ($\tau$ and
        $w$) do not move the level autocovariance at all, and the
        simulation truth switches the one that could ($z$) off.
\end{itemize}

\section{Model Setup and Notation}
\label{sec:model}

\subsection{Spatial skew-$t$ knot model}

Let $\{s_i\}_{i=1}^{n_s} \subset \R^2$ denote spatial locations and
$t \in \{1, \dots, T\}$ time indices. Fix $K$ knots. The conditional
model for the observed process is

\begin{equation}
  Y_t(s) = x_t(s)^\top \beta
         + \lambda \, z_{g(s, t), t}
         + \frac{1}{\sqrt{\tau_{g(s, t), t}}} \,
           \big[L_C \, \varepsilon_t\big]_s,
  \qquad \varepsilon_t \sim \Norm(0, I_{n_s}),
  \label{eq:kernel}
\end{equation}

where:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em, itemsep=0pt]
  \item $\beta \in \R^p$ are regression coefficients and $\lambda$ is
        the skewness parameter;
  \item $z_{k, t}, \tau_{k, t}$ are knot-level latent processes
        governing skew and scale;
  \item $w_{k, t} \in \R^2$ is the (possibly time-varying) location of
        knot $k$ at time $t$;
  \item $g(s, t) = \arg\min_k \|s - w_{k, t}\|$ is the knot membership
        function (a Voronoi assignment);
  \item $C = C(\rho, \nu, \gamma)$ is the spatial covariance matrix and
        $L_C = \operatorname{chol}(C)^\top$.
\end{itemize}

We collect the time-$t$ slice of all latents into
$\Ltot_t = (z_{\cdot, t}, \tau_{\cdot, t}, w_{\cdot, t})$ and the
fixed-parameter vector into $\theta$. Throughout we abbreviate the
conditional scale at a knot by
\begin{equation}
  \sigma_{k,t} := \tau_{k,t}^{-1/2},
  \label{eq:sigma-def}
\end{equation}
so that the third term of \eqref{eq:kernel} is
$\sigma_{g(s,t),t}\,[L_C\varepsilon_t]_s$.

\subsection{AR(2) standardisation}

Each scalar latent process (one per knot per quantity $z, \tau, w$) is
generated by

\begin{equation}
  X_t = \phi_1 X_{t-1} + \phi_2 X_{t-2} + \eta_t,
  \qquad \eta_t \sim \Norm(0, \sigma_\eta^2),
  \label{eq:ar2}
\end{equation}

with $(\phi_1, \phi_2)$ in the stationarity region. We adopt the
construction in \verb|simulate_ar2_standard()|, which fixes the
stationary variance and \emph{solves} for the innovation variance.

\begin{definition}[Standardised AR(2)]
\label{def:standardised}
Set $\gamma_0 = 1$, $\gamma_1 = \phi_1 / (1 - \phi_2)$, and
\begin{equation}
  \sigma_\eta^2
    = \gamma_0 - \phi_1 \gamma_1 - \phi_2 \gamma_2,
  \qquad \gamma_2 = \phi_1 \gamma_1 + \phi_2 \gamma_0.
  \label{eq:innovvar}
\end{equation}
The initial pair $(X_1, X_2)$ is drawn jointly from
$\Norm\!\big(0, [\begin{smallmatrix} 1 & \gamma_1 \\ \gamma_1 & 1
\end{smallmatrix}]\big)$, and the recursion~\eqref{eq:ar2} runs
forward for $t \ge 3$.
\end{definition}

\begin{assumption}[Stationarity]
\label{assn:stationary}
$(\phi_1, \phi_2)$ satisfies
$|\phi_2| < 1, \ \phi_1 + \phi_2 < 1, \ \phi_2 - \phi_1 < 1$,
so the roots of the AR(2) characteristic polynomial lie strictly
outside the unit circle.
\end{assumption}

\subsection{Prediction task}

The simstudy holdout fixes a subset $\mathcal S_{\mathrm{test}}$ of
test \emph{sites}; the training set is
$\D = \{Y_t(s) : s \notin \mathcal S_{\mathrm{test}}, \, t \in
\{1, \dots, T\}\}$, and the prediction targets are
$\{Y_t(s^*) : s^* \in \mathcal S_{\mathrm{test}}\}$. Every time point
is observed at every training site.

\begin{definition}[Brier score]
\label{def:brier}
For threshold $c$ and predictive probability
$\hat p(s^*, t; c) = \Pr(Y_t(s^*) > c \given \D)$,
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{BS}(c) = \E\big[(\indicator\{Y_t(s^*) > c\}
                        - \hat p(s^*, t; c))^2\big].
  \label{eq:brier}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\subsection{Gaussian surrogate for one latent series}
\label{subsec:surrogate}

Sections~\ref{sec:frozen}--\ref{sec:identifiability} use a linear
Gaussian surrogate that isolates a single knot's latent series.

\begin{definition}[Gaussian surrogate]
\label{def:surrogate}
Fix a knot $k$ and a latent quantity; write the standardised AR(2)
series as $X = (X_1, \dots, X_T)^\top$. The prior is
$X \sim \Norm(0, Q(\phi)^{-1})$, with $Q(\phi)$ the banded AR(2)
precision matrix, normalised so $\operatorname{diag}(Q(\phi)^{-1}) =
\mathbf 1$ (Definition~\ref{def:standardised}). After integrating the
remaining parameters, the time-$t$ training data act as a pseudo-%
observation
\begin{equation}
  d_t = X_t + e_t, \qquad e_t \sim \Norm(0, q_t^{-1}),
  \quad \text{independent across } t,
  \label{eq:pseudo}
\end{equation}
with $D_q = \operatorname{diag}(q_1, \dots, q_T)$. The posterior is
$X \mid d \sim \Norm(\mu(\phi), \Sigma(\phi))$,
\begin{equation}
  \Sigma(\phi) = \big(Q(\phi) + D_q\big)^{-1}, \qquad
  \mu(\phi) = \Sigma(\phi)\, D_q\, d.
  \label{eq:surrogate-post}
\end{equation}
We write $v_t(\phi) = [\Sigma(\phi)]_{tt}$ for the marginal posterior
variance and
$\kappa(\phi) = (1 + \phi_1^2 + \phi_2^2)/\sigma_\eta^2$
for the AR(2) full-conditional precision (an interior diagonal entry
of $Q(\phi)$).
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
The pseudo-observations \eqref{eq:pseudo} are independent across $t$
because the kernel~\eqref{eq:kernel} couples sites at a \emph{common}
$t$; the likelihood factorises over time given the latents. All
cross-time structure resides in the prior $Q(\phi)$.
\end{remark}

\section{Marginal Invariance of the Standardised AR(2)}
\label{sec:ar2-construction}

\begin{proposition}[Marginal invariance to $\phi$]
\label{prop:invariance}
Under Definition~\ref{def:standardised} and
Assumption~\ref{assn:stationary}, for every $t$,
$X_t \sim \Norm(0, 1)$ regardless of $(\phi_1, \phi_2)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By construction $X_1, X_2 \sim \Norm(0, 1)$ marginally. Suppose
inductively that $\Var(X_{t-1}) = \Var(X_{t-2}) = 1$ and
$\Cov(X_{t-1}, X_{t-2}) = \gamma_1$. Then from~\eqref{eq:ar2},
\[
  \Var(X_t)
    = \phi_1^2 + \phi_2^2 + 2 \phi_1 \phi_2 \gamma_1 + \sigma_\eta^2.
\]
Substituting~\eqref{eq:innovvar} and
$\gamma_2 = \phi_1 \gamma_1 + \phi_2$ gives $\Var(X_t) = 1$. The mean
is zero by linearity, and joint normality is preserved by the linear
recursion.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[AR(1) toy]
\label{cor:ar1}
For an AR(1) process standardised to $\gamma_0 = 1$,
$\sigma_\eta^2 = 1 - \phi^2$ and $X_t \sim \Norm(0, 1)$ for every
admissible $\phi$. The autocorrelation
$\Cov(X_t, X_{t+h}) = \phi^{|h|}$ is the only quantity that moves with
$\phi$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[Identifiability]
\label{rem:identifiability}
$\phi$ is identifiable only from \emph{joint} distributions across
time; the one-point marginal is $\phi$-blind. A single draw $X_t$
carries zero information about $\phi$. This remark is made quantitative
in Section~\ref{sec:identifiability}.
\end{remark}

\section{Why Brier Marginalises $\phi$ Away}
\label{sec:derivations}

\subsection{The predictive integral collapses to the time-$t$ marginal}

\begin{proposition}[Predictive at $(s^*, t)$ uses only time-$t$ latents]
\label{prop:kernel-decomposition}
For the kernel~\eqref{eq:kernel}, the conditional CDF at $(s^*, t)$
given $(\theta, \Ltot_t)$ does not depend on $\Ltot_{t'}$, $t' \ne t$.
Hence
\begin{equation}
  \hat p(s^*, t; c)
    = \int \!\!\int
        \Pr\!\big(Y_t(s^*) > c \given \theta, \Ltot_t\big) \,
        \pi(\Ltot_t \given \theta, \D) \,
        \pi(\theta \given \D) \, d\Ltot_t \, d\theta.
  \label{eq:predictive}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The right-hand side of~\eqref{eq:kernel} involves only $\Ltot_t$ at
the same $t$. Fubini and the factorisation
$\pi(\theta, \Ltot_t \given \D) = \pi(\Ltot_t \given \theta, \D)\,
\pi(\theta \given \D)$ give the displayed form.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[$\phi$-free prior marginal at one $t$]
\label{prop:phi-free}
Under Definition~\ref{def:standardised}, the \emph{prior} marginal of
$\Ltot_t$ at any single $t$ does not depend on
$\phi := (\phi_z, \phi_\tau, \phi_w)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Each component of $\Ltot_t$ is a fixed monotone transform (gamma
inverse copula for $\tau$, half-normal inverse copula for $z$, inverse
probit for $w$) of a standardised AR(2) latent. By
Proposition~\ref{prop:invariance} each latent has marginal
$\Norm(0,1)$ for every admissible $\phi$, and the fixed transforms
preserve this invariance.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The posterior is the only route]
\label{cor:posterior-route}
Combining Propositions~\ref{prop:kernel-decomposition} and
\ref{prop:phi-free}, $\phi$ affects $\hat p(s^*, t; c)$ only through
the posterior $\pi(\theta \given \D)$ and the conditional
$\pi(\Ltot_t \given \theta, \D)$. The likelihood $p(\D \given \theta)$
couples observations across time and so depends on $\phi$; the kernel
at the held-out cell does not.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{Four leakage channels}

We enumerate the channels through which $\phi$ can still move the
predictive at $(s^*, t)$.

\begin{description}[leftmargin=2em, itemsep=0.25em]
  \item[Channel A: $z_{g, t}$ via temporal pooling.] AR(2) tightens
    $\pi(z_{k, t} \given \D)$ by borrowing from $z_{k, t \pm 1},
    z_{k, t \pm 2}$. Bounded by the time-$t$ likelihood precision.
  \item[Channel B: $\tau_{g, t}$ via temporal pooling.] Symmetric to
    Channel A.
  \item[Channel C: knot membership $g(s^*, t)$.] AR(2) tightens
    $\pi(w_{k, t} \given \D)$, but $g(s^*, t)$ is a discrete
    $\arg\min$; the tightening propagates only when a Voronoi boundary
    crosses $s^*$. For $K = 1$ the channel is closed.
  \item[Channel D: spatial noise $L_C \varepsilon_t /
    \sqrt{\tau_{g,t}}$.] $\varepsilon_t$ is i.i.d.\ in $t$ given
    $\theta$; $\phi$ does not appear in this channel at all.
\end{description}

\begin{description}[leftmargin=2em, itemsep=0.25em]
  \item[Channel E (cost): posterior on $\phi$.] Methods 7 and 8 turn
    on AR(2) for $z, \tau, w$ simultaneously, introducing six
    parameters $\phi_{1z}, \phi_{2z}, \phi_{1\tau}, \phi_{2\tau},
    \phi_{1w}, \phi_{2w}$ whose joint posterior adds variability to
    \eqref{eq:predictive}.
\end{description}

\begin{lemma}[Predictive variance decomposition]
\label{lem:variance}
For the held-out predictive probability
$\hat F_{s^*, t}(c) = \hat p(s^*, t; c)$,
\begin{equation}
  \Var\!\big(\hat F_{s^*, t}(c)\big)
    \approx \E_\phi\!\big[\Var\!\big(\hat F \given \phi\big)\big]
          + \Var_\phi\!\big(\E[\hat F \given \phi]\big).
  \label{eq:variance-decomp}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

The AR(2) prior shrinks the first summand through Channels A--C and
introduces the second (Channel E), which is identically zero under
i.i.d.\ time. Brier is sensitive to the sum.

\section{A Perfectly Specified $\phi$ Still Cannot Dominate}
\label{sec:frozen}

We now ask whether a \emph{frozen, true} $\phi$ --- which eliminates
Channel E entirely --- would let AR(2) dominate. It does not.

\subsection{Brier decomposition}

\begin{lemma}[Reducible/irreducible split]
\label{lem:brier-split}
For a held-out cell, with $p(c) = \Pr(Y_t(s^*) > c)$ the true
exceedance probability,
\begin{equation}
  \E[\mathrm{BS}(c)]
    = \underbrace{p(c)\,(1 - p(c))}_{\text{irreducible}}
    + \underbrace{\big(\E[\hat p] - p(c)\big)^2 + \Var(\hat p)}
        _{\text{reducible}}.
  \label{eq:brier-split}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

The irreducible term is a property of the data-generating process and
is $\phi$-invariant; AR(2) can act only on the reducible term. With
$q_t$ training sites per knot informing each latent, the per-time
likelihood swamps the prior, so both the AR(2) and i.i.d.\ models are
unbiased to $O(q_t^{-1})$ and the contest reduces to $\Var(\hat p)$.

\subsection{The variance splits into a $\phi$-reducible part and a
$\phi$-invariant floor}

By the delta method, $\Var(\hat p) \approx [\partial_\mu \Pr(Y>c)]^2\,
\Var(Y_t(s^*) \given \D)$, and

\begin{equation}
  \Var\big(Y_t(s^*) \given \D\big)
    = \underbrace{\lambda^2\, v_t^z}_{\text{Ch.\ A}}
    + \underbrace{(\cdots)\, v_t^\tau}_{\text{Ch.\ B}}
    + \underbrace{\E[\tau_{g,t}^{-1} \given \D]\; C_{s^* s^*}}
        _{\text{Ch.\ D, } \phi\text{-invariant}}.
  \label{eq:var-split}
\end{equation}

Channel D is the variance of the i.i.d.-in-time innovation
$L_C \varepsilon_t$; it contains no $\phi$ and a perfect $\phi$ cannot
shrink it.

\subsection{The latent tightening is strictly positive but bounded}

\begin{proposition}[$O(q_t^{-2})$ bound on the frozen-$\phi$ benefit]
\label{prop:frozen-bound}
In the surrogate of Definition~\ref{def:surrogate} with constant
$q_t \equiv q$, let $v_t^{\mathrm{iid}} = (1+q)^{-1}$ be the i.i.d.\
posterior variance and $v_t(\phi)$ the AR(2) posterior variance. Then
\begin{equation}
  0 \;<\; \Delta v_t := v_t^{\mathrm{iid}} - v_t(\phi)
    \;\le\; \frac{\kappa(\phi) - 1}{(1 + q)\,(\kappa(\phi) + q)}
    \;=\; O\!\big(q^{-2}\big),
  \label{eq:frozen-bound}
\end{equation}
for every $\phi \ne 0$, where
$\kappa(\phi) = (1 + \phi_1^2 + \phi_2^2)/\sigma_\eta^2 > 1$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The i.i.d.\ prior contributes precision $1$ (the $\gamma_0 = 1$
normalisation), so $v_t^{\mathrm{iid}} = (1+q)^{-1}$. The AR(2)
posterior variance is bounded below by the case in which all
neighbours are known exactly, which contributes the full-conditional
precision $\kappa(\phi)$: $v_t(\phi) \ge (\kappa(\phi) + q)^{-1}$.
Subtracting gives the upper bound. Strict positivity follows because
$\sigma_\eta^2 \le 1$ forces $\kappa(\phi) \ge 1/\sigma_\eta^2 \ge 1$,
with equality iff $\phi = 0$; any nonzero $\phi$ with an informed
neighbour strictly tightens $v_t$. Expanding the bound for large $q$
gives $\Delta v_t \lesssim (\kappa(\phi)-1)/q^2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Frozen-$\phi$ verdict]
\label{cor:frozen-verdict}
A frozen true $\phi$ zeroes the second term of~\eqref{eq:variance-decomp}
but reduces the first only by $O(q^{-2})$, and only on the
Channels A/B components of~\eqref{eq:var-split}, never on the
$\phi$-invariant Channel-D floor. In the data-rich regime $q \gg 1$
the improvement is below the Monte Carlo resolution of a $50$-dataset
comparison: AR(2) fails to \emph{dominate} not because $\phi$ is
useless but because its benefit is bounded, partial, and crowded out.
\end{corollary}

% ====================================================================
\section{The Level-Autocovariance Ceiling}
\label{sec:level-autocov}

Section~\ref{sec:frozen} bounded the \emph{posterior-pooling} benefit
of a frozen $\phi$ through a Gaussian surrogate. We now give an exact,
model-level statement that does not rely on the surrogate: AR(2)
reaches the \emph{level} of $Y_t(s)$ through a single channel, and the
simulation truth closes even that channel.

\subsection{Exact decomposition of the level autocovariance}

Fix a held-out site $s^*$ with stable membership
$g(s^*, t) \equiv g$ over the window of interest, and condition on the
covariates so that $m_{s^*,t} := x_t(s^*)^\top\beta$ is deterministic.
The stochastic part of the level is, using \eqref{eq:sigma-def},
\begin{equation}
  \widetilde Y_t
    := Y_t(s^*) - m_{s^*,t}
     = \lambda\, z_{g,t} \;+\; \sigma_{g,t}\, u_t,
  \qquad u_t := \big[L_C\varepsilon_t\big]_{s^*},
  \label{eq:ytilde}
\end{equation}
where $\Var(u_t) = C_{s^*s^*}$, the $u_t$ are i.i.d.\ across $t$, and
$u \perp (z,\tau)$. The latent series $z$ and $\tau$ are driven by
separate AR(2) recursions and separate inverse-copula transforms,
hence $z \perp \tau$.

\begin{proposition}[Level autocovariance]
\label{prop:level-autocov}
Under model~\eqref{eq:kernel} with the independence structure above,
for every lag $h \ge 1$
\begin{equation}
  \Cov\big(\widetilde Y_t,\, \widetilde Y_{t+h}\big)
    \;=\; \lambda^2\,\gamma_z(h),
  \qquad
  \gamma_z(h) := \Cov\big(z_{g,t},\, z_{g,t+h}\big).
  \label{eq:level-autocov}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Expand the product of \eqref{eq:ytilde} at times $t$ and $t+h$ into
four terms:
\[
  \Cov(\widetilde Y_t,\widetilde Y_{t+h})
   = \lambda^2\Cov(z_{g,t},z_{g,t+h})
   + \lambda\,\Cov(z_{g,t},\sigma_{g,t+h}u_{t+h})
   + \lambda\,\Cov(\sigma_{g,t}u_t,z_{g,t+h})
   + \Cov(\sigma_{g,t}u_t,\sigma_{g,t+h}u_{t+h}).
\]
Both mixed terms vanish: $u$ has mean zero and is independent of
$(z,\sigma)$, so e.g.\ $\E[z_{g,t}\sigma_{g,t+h}u_{t+h}]
= \E[z_{g,t}\sigma_{g,t+h}]\,\E[u_{t+h}] = 0$, and the marginal factor
$\E[\sigma_{g,t+h}u_{t+h}]=0$ likewise. For the last term, $u\perp\sigma$
and the $u_t$ are i.i.d.\ mean-zero across $t$, so for $h\ge1$
\[
  \E[\sigma_{g,t}u_t\,\sigma_{g,t+h}u_{t+h}]
   = \E[\sigma_{g,t}\sigma_{g,t+h}]\;
     \E[u_t]\,\E[u_{t+h}] = 0,
\]
and the product of marginal means is zero as well. Only the first term
survives.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The $\tau$-AR(2) is pure stochastic volatility]
\label{cor:tau-volatility}
The AR(2) on $\tau$ contributes \emph{zero} to the level
autocovariance~\eqref{eq:level-autocov}. Its temporal structure
appears only in second-order moments: for $h\ge1$,
\begin{equation}
  \Cov\big(\widetilde Y_t^{\,2},\,\widetilde Y_{t+h}^{\,2}\big)
   \;\supseteq\;
   C_{s^*s^*}^2\;
   \Cov\big(\sigma_{g,t}^2,\,\sigma_{g,t+h}^2\big)
   \;\ne\; 0,
  \label{eq:vol-cluster}
\end{equation}
since $\sigma_{g,t}^2 = \tau_{g,t}^{-1}$ inherits the AR(2) dependence
of $\tau$. Hence $\tau$-AR(2) is invisible to any scoring rule that is
a functional of the predictive law of the \emph{level} $Y$, and
visible only to rules sensitive to $|Y|^p$ (volatility clustering).
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[The $w$-channel is second order]
\label{cor:w-channel}
The AR(2) on knot locations $w$ enters \eqref{eq:level-autocov} only
through the membership map $g(s^*,t)$. Under stable membership its
contribution is zero; it becomes active only on the event that a
Voronoi boundary sweeps across $s^*$, a second-order effect that is
moreover absent for $K=1$.
\end{corollary}

Propositions~\ref{prop:level-autocov} and its corollaries isolate the
single level-channel: \emph{the AR(2) prior moves the temporal
autocovariance of the observable only through $\lambda z$.}

\subsection{The temporal signal-to-noise ratio and the CRPS ceiling}

\begin{definition}[Temporal signal-to-noise ratio]
\label{def:snr}
With $\Var(\widetilde Y_t) = \lambda^2\Var(z) + \E[\sigma^2]\,
C_{s^*s^*}$, define
\begin{equation}
  \SNR \;:=\; \frac{\lambda^2\,\Var(z)}
                   {\lambda^2\,\Var(z) + \E[\sigma^2]\,C_{s^*s^*}}
  \;\in\;[0,1],
  \label{eq:snr}
\end{equation}
the share of level variance carried by the AR-bearing skew channel.
The \emph{forecastable fraction} at horizon $h$ is
\begin{equation}
  R(h) \;:=\; 1 - \frac{\Var(\widetilde Y_{t+h}\given\text{past})}
                       {\Var(\widetilde Y_{t+h})}
        \;=\; \SNR \cdot \kappa_z(h),
  \label{eq:Rh}
\end{equation}
where $\kappa_z(h) := 1 - \Var(z_{t+h}\given z_{\le t})/\Var(z)
\in[0,1]$ is the AR(2) forecastable variance fraction of $z$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[CRPS ceiling]
\label{prop:crps-ceiling}
Suppose the predictive law of the level is approximately Gaussian, so
that for a calibrated forecast $\E\,\mathrm{CRPS} = \sigma_{\mathrm{pred}}
/\sqrt{\pi}$. Conditioning on the past reduces the predictive standard
deviation from $\sigma_Y$ (the marginal, no-AR model) to
$\sigma_Y\sqrt{1-R(h)}$. Hence the best-case relative improvement of
the AR(2) model over the i.i.d.-in-time model is
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\Delta\,\mathrm{CRPS}}{\mathrm{CRPS}}
    \;=\; 1 - \sqrt{1 - R(h)}.
  \label{eq:crps-ceiling}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For $Y\sim\Norm(\mu,\sigma^2)$ the closed form of the CRPS gives
$\E_{Y}\mathrm{CRPS}(\Norm(\mu,\sigma^2),Y) = \sigma/\sqrt\pi$, linear
in $\sigma$. The marginal model predicts with standard deviation
$\sigma_Y$; the AR(2) model, conditioning on the past, predicts with
$\sigma_Y\sqrt{1-R(h)}$ by~\eqref{eq:Rh}. The ratio of expected scores
is $\sqrt{1-R(h)}$ and the relative improvement is its complement.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[There is no universal threshold]
\label{rem:no-threshold}
A value such as $\SNR\approx0.1$ is \emph{not} a constant from the
spatial-extremes literature; it is simply the regime in which the
best-case relative CRPS gain at $h=1$ is low single digits
(Table~\ref{tab:crps-ceiling}), comparable to the Monte Carlo standard
error of a few dozen holdout blocks. Whether a gain is detectable is
the separate calculation of Section~\ref{subsec:detectability}, not a
comparison against a magic number. The Brier score is strictly weaker
than CRPS here: it binarises at a single threshold $\{Y>c\}$ and so
sees the temporal signal through one crossing only --- a further
information loss on top of the ceiling~\eqref{eq:crps-ceiling}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The simulation truth sets the ceiling to zero}

\begin{corollary}[Level whiteness under the experiment's truth]
\label{cor:sim-truth-zero}
The data-generating process of settings 9--10 sets
$\phi_z = (0,0)$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:adversarial} below). Then $z$ is
i.i.d.\ in time, $\gamma_z(h) = 0$ and $\kappa_z(h) = 0$ for all
$h\ge1$, so by Proposition~\ref{prop:level-autocov} and
Definition~\ref{def:snr},
\begin{equation}
  \Cov\big(\widetilde Y_t,\widetilde Y_{t+h}\big) = 0,
  \qquad R(h) = 0
  \qquad\text{for all } h\ge1.
\end{equation}
The level of $Y$ is \emph{exactly white in time}. No level-based
scoring rule --- Brier, $\sum$CRPS, or the energy score on levels ---
can reward the AR(2) prior, for any holdout design and for any
specification of $\phi$ (estimated, true-initialised, or frozen).
\end{corollary}

This is the exact counterpart of Corollary~\ref{cor:frozen-verdict}:
not merely \emph{the benefit is small} but \emph{the level-channel
benefit is identically zero under the experiment's own truth}. The
true temporal structure of settings 9--10 lives entirely in $\tau$
(volatility, Corollary~\ref{cor:tau-volatility}) and $w$ (membership
drift, Corollary~\ref{cor:w-channel}), and is reachable only by
volatility- or dependence-sensitive scores
(Section~\ref{sec:redesign}).

\begin{remark}[Two confounds to control]
\label{rem:confounds}
First, $\gamma_z(h)$ in~\eqref{eq:level-autocov} is the autocovariance
of the \emph{transformed} $z$ (post inverse-copula); since the
transform is a fixed monotone map of the standardised AR(2) latent
$z^\star$, $\gamma_z(h)$ inherits the geometric decay of $z^\star$ but
with a distorted magnitude --- the diagnostic of
Appendix~\ref{app:acf-diagnostic} measures it directly rather than
assuming it. Second, if the covariate $x_t(s^*)$ itself carries
temporal autocorrelation, part of any observed
$\Cov(Y_t,Y_{t+h})$ is covariate-driven and must be removed by
conditioning on $x$ before attributing a score difference to AR(2).
\end{remark}

\subsection{Conditional stiffness versus marginal softness}

\begin{proposition}[Frozen $\phi$ targets a stiffer chain]
\label{prop:stiff}
Single-site updates of the latent field explore the conditional
posterior $\pi(X \given \phi, \D)$; a sampler that also updates $\phi$
explores the marginal
$\pi(X \given \D) = \int \pi(X \given \phi, \D)\,\pi(\phi \given \D)\,
d\phi$. The two are related by
\begin{equation}
  \Var(X \given \D)
    = \E_\phi\!\big[\Var(X \given \phi, \D)\big]
    + \Var_\phi\!\big(\E[X \given \phi, \D]\big),
  \label{eq:cond-marg}
\end{equation}
so the marginal target is \emph{softer} (larger variance) in the
smooth low-frequency directions than the conditional target at any
fixed $\phi$, including $\phi = \phi_{\mathrm{true}}$.
\end{proposition}

The banded precision $Q(\phi)$ has small eigenvalues on its smooth
eigenvectors; at fixed persistent $\phi$ the conditional posterior of
$X$ is strongly internally correlated along those modes, and
single-site updates crawl. Freezing $\phi$ --- even at the truth ---
locks the chain onto this stiff slice; updating $\phi$ targets the
softer marginal of~\eqref{eq:cond-marg}.

\subsection{$\phi$ as a parameter-expansion coordinate}

\begin{remark}[Compensating degree of freedom]
\label{rem:param-expansion}
The latent field's smoothness is a nuisance that $\tau$, the knots,
and $\beta$ all interact with. When $\phi$ is frozen, the smoothness
is a hard constraint and a $\tau$/knot proposal that would prefer a
smoother field is rejected; when $\phi$ is free, $\phi$ absorbs the
coordinated adjustment and the proposal is accepted. This is the
mechanism of parameter-expanded data augmentation~\cite{liu1999px}:
an extra coordinate aligned with a stiff direction improves the
conditioning of the sampler for every coupled block. The
centered/non-centered and interweaving literature
\cite{prs2007, yu2011interweaving} describes the same phenomenon.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Two roles of $\phi$]
\label{rem:two-roles}
$\phi$ has a role as an \emph{estimand} --- whose point value barely
affects the held-out Brier (Sections~\ref{sec:derivations}--%
\ref{sec:level-autocov}) --- and a role as a \emph{mixing coordinate}
--- whose \emph{freedom to move} conditions the sampler for $\tau$, the
knots, and $z$. Table~\ref{tab:phi-strategies} is explained by the
second role: ``frozen at truth'' is not ``free.'' Updating $\phi$ is
also fastest in wall-clock because $\phi$ is low-dimensional, so the
mixing gain is bought at negligible per-iteration cost.
\end{remark}

\section{A Misspecified $\phi$: Robust Variance, Fragile Bias}
\label{sec:misspecified}

We now treat the opposite extreme: $\phi$ estimated far from the truth.

\subsection{The variance diagnostic is robust to a wrong $\phi$}

\begin{proposition}[Posterior variance depends only on the assumed
$\phi$]
\label{prop:variance-only}
In the surrogate of Definition~\ref{def:surrogate}, $v_t(\phi) =
[\Sigma(\phi)]_{tt}$ depends on the \emph{assumed} $\phi$ and $D_q$
only --- not on the true $\phi^\ast$. Moreover
\begin{equation}
  v_t(\phi) = \Var(X_t \given d_{1:T})
    \;\le\; \Var(X_t \given d_t) = \frac{1}{1 + q_t}
    = v_t^{\mathrm{iid}}
  \label{eq:variance-monotone}
\end{equation}
for every stationary $\phi$, with strict inequality whenever
$\phi \ne 0$ and some neighbour carries data.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$v_t(\phi)$ is a function of $Q(\phi) + D_q$ only. Under the AR prior
$X_t$ is correlated with $d_{t'}$, $t' \ne t$, so conditioning on those
extra pseudo-observations cannot increase the variance; under the
i.i.d.\ prior $X_t \perp d_{t'}$ and they are inert, giving
$v_t^{\mathrm{iid}} = (1+q_t)^{-1}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The SD plot certifies coupling, not correctness]
\label{cor:sd-plot}
A per-cell posterior-SD plot of $X_{k,t}$ shows
$\sqrt{v_t(\phi)} < \sqrt{v_t^{\mathrm{iid}}}$ for \emph{any} nonzero
$\phi$, with the largest relative reduction at data-poor cells (small
$q_t$). The plot is a diagnostic that the coupling is \emph{active},
not that $\phi$ is \emph{correct}.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{Bias is where a wrong $\phi$ is punished}

Using $\Sigma(\phi) D_q = I - \Sigma(\phi) Q(\phi)$, the posterior
mean has conditional bias
\begin{equation}
  \E[\mu(\phi) \given X] - X = -\,\Sigma(\phi)\, Q(\phi)\, X,
  \label{eq:bias-operator}
\end{equation}
so a smoother built on the wrong $\phi$ shrinks $X$ along the wrong
correlation directions. The reconstruction risk

\begin{equation}
  R_t(\phi; \phi^\ast)
    = \E\big[(\mu_t(\phi) - X_t)^2\big]
    = R_t(\phi^\ast; \phi^\ast)
    + \tfrac12 (\phi - \phi^\ast)^\top H_t (\phi - \phi^\ast)
    + o\big(\|\phi - \phi^\ast\|^2\big)
  \label{eq:risk-expansion}
\end{equation}

is minimised at $\phi = \phi^\ast$. Small misspecification costs only
second order (robust); a large or wrong-sign $\phi$ can push
$R_t(\phi;\phi^\ast)$ above the i.i.d.\ risk even while, by
Proposition~\ref{prop:variance-only}, the SD plot still looks tighter.

\subsection{Collapse to i.i.d.\ occurs only as $\phi \to 0$}

\begin{proposition}[Collapse condition]
\label{prop:collapse}
As $\phi \to 0$, $Q(\phi) \to I$, $\Sigma(\phi) \to (I + D_q)^{-1}$
(diagonal), $\mu(\phi) \to$ the i.i.d.\ posterior mean, and
$v_t(\phi) \to v_t^{\mathrm{iid}}$. AR(2) degenerates to i.i.d.\
\emph{continuously and only} in this limit; a merely wrong but nonzero
$\phi$ keeps the coupling fully active.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[Weak identifiability resurrects Channel E]
\label{rem:bma}
If $\pi(\phi \given \D)$ is wide, the predictive averages smoothers:
\begin{equation}
  v_t^{\mathrm{BMA}}
    = \underbrace{\E_{\phi \given \D}[v_t(\phi)]}
        _{\le\, v_t^{\mathrm{iid}}}
    + \underbrace{\Var_{\phi \given \D}(\mu_t(\phi))}
        _{\text{Channel E, reborn}}.
  \label{eq:bma}
\end{equation}
Weak identifiability either collapses to i.i.d.\ (if $\pi(\phi)$ pulls
to $0$) or keeps the coupling on while reinflating the
estimation-variance cost.
\end{remark}

% ====================================================================
\section{The Mechanism of the Observed Collapse $\hat\phi \to 0$}
\label{sec:identifiability}

The empirical posteriors show Case~1 of Remark~\ref{rem:bma}:
$\hat\phi$ shrinks aggressively to $0$ even though the data-generating
process carries a true temporal signal,
$\phi_\tau = (0.8, -0.6)$. We derive why.

\subsection{$\phi$-information is monotone increasing in $q_t$}

\begin{proposition}[Marginal likelihood of $\phi$]
\label{prop:fisher-monotone}
In the surrogate of Definition~\ref{def:surrogate}, the marginal
likelihood of $\phi$ from one knot series is
\begin{equation}
  2 \log L(\phi)
    = \log|Q(\phi)| - \log|Q(\phi) + D_q|
    + d^\top D_q (Q(\phi) + D_q)^{-1} D_q\, d + \mathrm{const}.
  \label{eq:marginal-likelihood}
\end{equation}
Its limiting behaviour is
\begin{align}
  D_q \to 0:    &\quad 2\log L(\phi) \to \mathrm{const}
                  \quad (\phi \text{ unidentified}); \\
  D_q \to \infty:&\quad 2\log L(\phi) \to
                  \log|Q(\phi)| - X^\top Q(\phi)\, X
                  \quad (\text{complete-data AR(2) likelihood}).
\end{align}
The Fisher information $\mathcal I(\phi)$ interpolates monotonically
between the two: richer per-time data \emph{increases} the information
about $\phi$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Equation~\eqref{eq:marginal-likelihood} is the standard Gaussian
integral $\int \exp(-\tfrac12 (X-d)^\top D_q (X-d) - \tfrac12 X^\top
Q(\phi) X)\, dX$. For $D_q \to 0$ the determinant terms cancel and the
quadratic form vanishes. For $D_q \to \infty$, $(Q+D_q)^{-1} \to
D_q^{-1} - D_q^{-1} Q D_q^{-1}$ and $d \to X$, leaving the complete-data
log-likelihood.
\end{proof}

The hypothesis ``rich $q_t$ masks the temporal linkage'' is thus
inverted: rich $q_t$ is the only thing that reveals $\phi$. A collapse
$\hat\phi \to 0$ implies the \emph{effective} $q_t$ for the relevant
latent is small.

\subsection{Errors-in-variables attenuation}

\begin{lemma}[Attenuation of the visible autocorrelation]
\label{lem:attenuation}
The pseudo-data series of Definition~\ref{def:surrogate} has
covariance $\Cov(d) = Q(\phi^\ast)^{-1} + D_q^{-1}$, i.e.\ AR(2)
\emph{plus white noise}. With constant $q_t \equiv q$, its lag-$h$
autocorrelation is
\begin{equation}
  \rho_h^{\mathrm{visible}}
    = \frac{\gamma_h}{\gamma_0 + q^{-1}}
    = \underbrace{\frac{q}{q + 1}}_{\text{attenuation } a}\;
      \rho_h^{\mathrm{true}}, \qquad h \ge 1.
  \label{eq:attenuation}
\end{equation}
Fitting the pure AR(2) model recovers $\phi$ from the attenuated
autocorrelations; as $a \to 0$, $\hat\phi \to 0$.
\end{lemma}

For $\phi_\tau = (0.8, -0.6)$ the true lag-1 autocorrelation is
$\gamma_1 = \phi_{1\tau}/(1 - \phi_{2\tau}) = 0.8/1.6 = 0.5$. With $K$
knot series of length $T$, $\mathrm{SE}(\hat\rho_1) \approx (KT)^{-1/2}$,
so $\phi$ is statistically alive only if

\begin{equation}
  \frac{q}{q+1}\,\gamma_1 \;\gtrsim\; \frac{1}{\sqrt{KT}}
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  q \;\gtrsim\; 0.15
  \qquad (\gamma_1 = 0.5,\ K = 5,\ T = 50).
  \label{eq:collapse-threshold}
\end{equation}

An aggressive collapse therefore implies an effective $q_t^\tau$ of
order $0.1$--$0.3$.

\subsection{Why $q_t^\tau$ is structurally small}

The raw site count is not the effective precision for a latent
\emph{scale}. Six losses compound:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em, itemsep=0pt]
  \item scale parameters carry less Fisher information than means;
  \item sites in a knot cell are spatially correlated through
        $C(\rho, \nu, \gamma)$, so the effective sample size is far
        below the cell count;
  \item residual dispersion is jointly explained by $\tau$, $C$,
        $\nu$, and $z$ --- the information is split;
  \item $\tau$ is the mixing variable of the $t$-distribution, weakly
        identified per draw;
  \item the gamma inverse-copula layer between $\tau$ and $\tau^\star$
        loses further information;
  \item knot membership $g(s,t)$ drifts in $t$, so the informing site
        set is unstable.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{The Gibbs feedback turns attenuation into collapse}

\begin{remark}[Fixed point of the Gibbs map]
\label{rem:gibbs-fixed-point}
The two full conditionals form a self-reinforcing map:
\[
  \phi \given X \;\longrightarrow\; \text{small if } X \text{ looks
  rough}, \qquad
  X \given \phi \;\longrightarrow\; \text{rough if } \phi \text{ is
  small}.
\]
If $\phi \approx 0$, the smoother is i.i.d., each $X_t$ is pulled only
by its noisy $d_t$, the realised series has attenuated autocorrelation
$a\,\gamma_1$, and $\pi(\phi \given X)$ concentrates near $0$ again.
$\phi \approx 0$ is a stable fixed point; a prior centred at or diffuse
around $0$ selects its basin. That a true-initialised chain also
collapses indicates the \emph{marginal} posterior --- not merely the
basin --- favours small $\phi$.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The data-generating process is adversarial}

The truth places the temporal signal in exactly the latents with the
weakest per-time information:
\begin{equation}
  \phi_z = (0, 0), \qquad
  \phi_\tau = (0.8, -0.6), \qquad
  \phi_w = (0.9, 0).
  \label{eq:adversarial}
\end{equation}
The latent that \emph{would} be well identified is $z$ --- a
location-type shift with large $q_t^z$ --- and its true $\phi$ is
exactly zero. The signal lives in $\tau$ (a scale, tiny $q_t^\tau$)
and $w$ (a knot location read through a discrete $\arg\min$, tiny
$q_t^w$). Combined with Corollary~\ref{cor:sim-truth-zero}, this is
doubly adversarial: the one latent whose AR(2) would have reached the
\emph{level} of $Y$ is precisely the one the truth switches off.

\section{Scoring Redesign and Evaluation Methodology}
\label{sec:redesign}

The diagnosis above dictates the remedy. The level of $Y$ is white in
time (Corollary~\ref{cor:sim-truth-zero}), so a level-based score on a
spatial holdout cannot reward AR(2). Three things must change: the
holdout geometry, the scoring rule, and the marginal scale on which it
acts.

\subsection{Averaged CRPS cannot see dependence}
\label{subsec:avg-crps}

\begin{proposition}[Averaged CRPS is proper for marginals only]
\label{prop:avg-crps}
Let $\overline{\mathrm{CRPS}}(F) = \tfrac{1}{n_s T}\sum_{s,t}
\mathrm{CRPS}(F_{s,t}, y_{s,t})$, where $F_{s,t}$ is the predictive
marginal at cell $(s,t)$. Then $\overline{\mathrm{CRPS}}$ is strictly
proper for the collection of marginals $\{F_{s,t}\}$ but \emph{not}
strictly proper for the joint law $F$: any two joint forecasts with
identical marginals receive identical expected
$\overline{\mathrm{CRPS}}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$\mathrm{CRPS}(F_{s,t},\cdot)$ is a functional of the marginal
$F_{s,t}$ alone. Its expectation under the true joint law depends only
on the true marginals; hence every joint forecast whose marginals
match the truth attains the same minimal expected score, irrespective
of its copula.
\end{proof}

Consequently $\overline{\mathrm{CRPS}}$ is the correct
\emph{marginal-calibration control} but is blind to the cross-time and
cross-site dependence that AR(2) is meant to supply. The dependence
test must come from a genuinely multivariate score
(Section~\ref{subsec:multivariate}).

\subsection{Design A: block-time-forecast holdout}
\label{subsec:designA}

Hold out a \emph{terminal} block $\mathcal B = \{T-T_h+1,\dots,T\}$ and
train on $\{Y_t(s):t\le T-T_h\}$. The predictive at horizon
$t^*\in\mathcal B$ requires the AR(2) conditional
\begin{equation}
  z_{k,t^*}\given\D \sim
    \Norm\!\big(\phi_1\hat z_{k,t^*-1}+\phi_2\hat z_{k,t^*-2},\;
                \sigma_\eta^2+\text{filter uncertainty}\big),
  \label{eq:forecast-conditional}
\end{equation}
which is strictly $\phi$-dependent --- unlike the spatial holdout, the
temporal channel is now load-bearing.

\begin{remark}[Implementing the predictive loop]
\label{rem:predictive-loop}
Two implementations target different distributions. Treating
$\mathcal B$ as missing data inside the Gibbs sampler samples
$p(y_\mathcal{B}\given y_\mathcal{O}) = \int p(y_\mathcal{B}\given
\theta,\Ltot)\,p(\theta,\Ltot\given y_\mathcal{O})\,d(\theta,\Ltot)$.
For a \emph{terminal} block this integral \emph{is} the forecast
distribution and in-sampler imputation coincides with a correct forward
simulation. For an \emph{interior} block it conditions on future
observations and yields a \emph{smoothing} distribution, which is not a
forecast and flatters the AR(2) model unfairly. Recommendation: hold
out a terminal block and use the model's missing-data imputation inside
Gibbs --- it propagates $(\theta,\Ltot)$ uncertainty jointly. A
post-hoc forward simulation is also valid but must, per posterior draw,
run the AR(2) recursions of \verb|rpotspatTS_arp()| forward from the
drawn terminal latent states; substituting posterior means collapses
$\Var(y_\mathcal{B})$ and corrupts every score.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Per-horizon scoring and effective memory}
\label{subsec:per-horizon}

AR(2) forecast skill decays geometrically. Let $\rho^\ast =
\max_i|\lambda_i|$ be the dominant modulus of the AR(2) companion
matrix; then the forecastable variance fraction obeys
\begin{equation}
  \kappa_z(h) \;\asymp\; (\rho^\ast)^{2h},
  \qquad
  h^\ast \;:=\; \frac{\log(0.05)}{2\log\rho^\ast},
  \label{eq:effective-memory}
\end{equation}
with $h^\ast$ the \emph{effective memory} (where $\kappa_z$ falls to
$0.05$): $\rho^\ast=0.6\Rightarrow h^\ast\approx3$,
$\rho^\ast=0.8\Rightarrow h^\ast\approx7$. Two consequences:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.6em, itemsep=2pt]
  \item Choose the block length $T_h\approx2\text{--}3\,h^\ast$ --- long
        enough to see the decay, short enough that informative horizons
        are not diluted by null ones.
  \item \emph{Score per horizon}, reporting $S(h)$ as a curve in $h$
        rather than a single pooled number; pooling
        $T_h=50$ averages $\sim5$ informative horizons with $\sim45$
        null ones. Overlay the ceiling $1-\sqrt{1-R(h)}$ from
        Proposition~\ref{prop:crps-ceiling} as the theoretical bound.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Multivariate scores: energy and variogram}
\label{subsec:multivariate}

Score the held-out block as a single vector
$\bm y\in\R^{d}$, $d = n_s\cdot T_h$. The energy score
\cite{gneiting2007}
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{ES}(F, \bm y)
    = \E_F \|X - \bm y\|_2 - \tfrac12 \E_F \|X - X'\|_2,
  \qquad X, X' \overset{\text{iid}}{\sim} F,
  \label{eq:energy-score}
\end{equation}
is the multivariate generalisation of the CRPS ($\mathrm{CRPS}$ is the
$d=1$ case) and is consistent, but has low power to detect misspecified
dependence once the marginals are correct. The variogram score of
order $p$ \cite{scheuerer2015}
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{VS}_p(F, \bm y)
    = \sum_{i<j} w_{ij}
      \big( |y_i - y_j|^p - \E_F |X_i - X_j|^p \big)^2
  \label{eq:variogram-score}
\end{equation}
is built from pairwise differences and is the sensitive dependence
diagnostic. Recommendations, following \cite{scheuerer2015,
jordan2019}:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.6em, itemsep=2pt]
  \item Use $p=0.5$: the choice $p=2$ is dominated by the largest
        pairs (a few extreme cells swamp the signal), whereas $p=0.5$
        is robust under heavy tails.
  \item Weight $w_{ij}$ by inverse space--time separation, e.g.\
        $w_{ij} = (1 + \alpha\|s_i-s_j\|^2 + \beta(t_i-t_j)^2)^{-1}$,
        to downweight uninformative far pairs.
  \item Compute via the unbiased $U$-statistic estimators in the
        \texttt{scoringRules} package (\texttt{vs\_sample},
        \texttt{es\_sample}, \texttt{crps\_sample}) \cite{jordan2019}
        rather than a naive plug-in, which is biased.
  \item For an extremes-focused question, weight the score toward the
        tail with a threshold-weighted kernel \cite{allen2023}; condition
        with care, since na\"ively subsetting on the realised event
        breaks propriety \cite{lerch2017, diks2011}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Marginal standardisation: the PIT transform}
\label{subsec:pit}

Both \eqref{eq:energy-score} and \eqref{eq:variogram-score} are
sensitive to marginal scale; without standardisation a few
high-variance sites dominate and the score conflates marginal fit with
dependence fit. Apply the probability integral transform (PIT)
$u_{s,t} = \hat F_{s,t}(y_{s,t})$ cell-by-cell before scoring.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.6em, itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Uniform} margins isolate dependence; recommended as the
        primary scale, since the scientific question is whether AR(2)
        improves the joint structure.
  \item \textbf{Gumbel} margins retain comparable magnitude information
        across sites with a light enough tail to keep
        \eqref{eq:variogram-score} finite; a useful secondary check.
  \item \textbf{Fr\'echet} margins are the native max-stable scale but
        a poor scoring scale --- the heavy tail lets a single extreme
        dominate even at $p=0.5$; avoid unless scoring a max-stable
        model directly.
\end{itemize}

\begin{lemma}[PIT degeneracy]
\label{lem:pit-degeneracy}
If $\hat F_{s,t}$ is taken as the empirical CDF of the same $m$
posterior predictive draws that are then scored, the PIT-transformed
draws are the ranks $\{1/m,\dots,1\}$ --- a deterministic set
independent of the dependence structure, so the score cannot
discriminate any two methods.
\end{lemma}

The fix is to define $\hat F_{s,t}$ from a source disjoint from the
scored sample: either (a) split the predictive draws, using a held-back
subsample to estimate $\hat F_{s,t}$ and the remainder to score, or
(b) use a parametric tail surrogate --- fit a GPD or skew-$t$ to the
upper-tail draws and use that smooth CDF for both the observation and
the scored draws. Compute $\hat F_{s,t}$ once per
$(s,t,\text{method})$ and cache it.

\subsection{Design C (recommended): combined holdout + score triple}
\label{subsec:designC}

Hold out a set of test sites \emph{and} a terminal time block at the
remaining sites, PIT-transform to uniform margins, and report a
\emph{triple}:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em, itemsep=0pt]
  \item $\overline{\mathrm{CRPS}}$ on $(s^*,t)$, $t\le T-T_h$ --- the
        marginal-calibration control (Proposition~\ref{prop:avg-crps});
  \item per-horizon energy score on training sites over
        $t>T-T_h$ --- the temporal-coupling test;
  \item per-horizon variogram score $\mathrm{VS}_{0.5}$ on
        $(s^*,t>T-T_h)$ --- space-and-time dependence.
\end{enumerate}
The attribution that has power: if $\overline{\mathrm{CRPS}}$ is equal
across methods but $\mathrm{VS}_{0.5}$ differs, the difference is
\emph{purely dependence}. Because the level is white
(Corollary~\ref{cor:sim-truth-zero}), the only score in the triple
that can reward the true ($\tau,w$) structure is one applied to
$|Y|^p$ or to volatility-sensitive functionals; a
$\mathrm{VS}_{p}$ with $p\approx1$ on the levels, or any score on
$Y^2$, is the route to the $\tau$-AR(2) signal
(Corollary~\ref{cor:tau-volatility}).

\subsection{Detectability and reporting}
\label{subsec:detectability}

Let $\Delta = S_{\text{no-AR}} - S_{\text{AR}}$ be the paired score
difference, averaged over $N$ holdout blocks. Its standard error
scales as $\mathrm{SE}(\Delta)\propto N^{-1/2}$, and the detectability
index is $\Delta/\mathrm{SE}(\Delta)$. The expected $\Delta$ on the
CRPS is bounded by the ceiling
$[1-\sqrt{1-R(h)}]\cdot\mathrm{CRPS}_{\text{no-AR}}$ of
Proposition~\ref{prop:crps-ceiling}. Practical rule: from a pilot
estimate of $\SNR$ and $\kappa_z(h)$, compute the ceiling, then choose
$N$ (via rolling-origin blocks) so that $\mathrm{SE}(\Delta)$ sits a
few times below it. Report paired differences with bootstrap
intervals, per horizon, and a score-per-CPU-hour axis --- methods 7
and 8 are the more expensive.

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em, itemsep=2pt]
  \item The AR(2) standardisation makes the prior time-$t$ marginal of
        every latent $\phi$-invariant
        (Propositions~\ref{prop:invariance}, \ref{prop:phi-free}); the
        spatial-only Brier integrates the kernel against that marginal
        (Proposition~\ref{prop:kernel-decomposition}), so the temporal
        coupling marginalises out.
  \item Even a frozen true $\phi$ cannot dominate: the posterior-pooling
        benefit is $O(q_t^{-2})$ and never touches the $\phi$-invariant
        Channel-D floor (Proposition~\ref{prop:frozen-bound},
        Corollary~\ref{cor:frozen-verdict}).
  \item AR(2) reaches the \emph{level} of $Y$ through one channel only,
        $\Cov(\widetilde Y_t,\widetilde Y_{t+h})=\lambda^2\gamma_z(h)$
        (Proposition~\ref{prop:level-autocov}); the $\tau$-AR(2) is
        pure stochastic volatility (Corollary~\ref{cor:tau-volatility}).
        Because the simulation truth sets $\phi_z=(0,0)$, the level is
        exactly white in time and no level-based score can reward AR(2)
        for any holdout design (Corollary~\ref{cor:sim-truth-zero});
        the achievable gain is capped by $\SNR$ with the closed-form
        CRPS ceiling $1-\sqrt{1-R(h)}$
        (Proposition~\ref{prop:crps-ceiling}).
  \item A freely mixing $\phi$ helps the \emph{other} parameters
        converge by softening a stiff conditional target and acting as
        a parameter-expansion coordinate
        (Proposition~\ref{prop:stiff}, Remarks~\ref{rem:param-expansion}--%
        \ref{rem:two-roles}).
  \item A misspecified $\phi$ still reduces posterior variance --- the
        SD plot is robust to a wrong $\phi$
        (Proposition~\ref{prop:variance-only}) --- but pays in bias;
        collapse to i.i.d.\ occurs only as $\phi \to 0$
        (Proposition~\ref{prop:collapse}).
  \item The observed collapse $\hat\phi \to 0$ is errors-in-variables
        attenuation (Lemma~\ref{lem:attenuation}): $\phi$-information
        is monotone increasing in $q_t$
        (Proposition~\ref{prop:fisher-monotone}), and the simulation
        truth places the signal in the lowest-$q_t$ latents
        (Eq.~\eqref{eq:adversarial}).
\end{itemize}

Future work, in order of priority:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em, itemsep=0pt]
  \item \textbf{Compute the ceiling before re-scoring.} From the true
        parameters of settings 9--10, evaluate
        $\SNR=\lambda^2\Var(z)/\Var(\widetilde Y)$ and $\kappa_z(h)$
        (Definition~\ref{def:snr}); overlay $1-\sqrt{1-R(h)}$ on any
        per-horizon score curve as the theoretical bound. If the
        ceiling never exceeds $\mathrm{SE}(\Delta)$, that is itself the
        publishable result.
  \item \textbf{Run the four-layer ACF diagnostic}
        (Appendix~\ref{app:acf-diagnostic}) to confirm where the AR(2)
        signal is lost --- source latent, membership reindexing,
        $\lambda$-gating, or the i.i.d.\ spatial noise.
  \item \textbf{Implement Design C} in \texttt{scores.R} and
        \texttt{run-settings.R}: a terminal-block holdout, the
        predictive loop of Remark~\ref{rem:predictive-loop}, PIT
        standardisation (Section~\ref{subsec:pit}), and
        \texttt{scoringRules::\{es,vs,crps\}\_sample}.
  \item \textbf{Score volatility, not just level.} Since the true
        temporal signal of settings 9--10 lives in $\tau$
        (Corollary~\ref{cor:tau-volatility}), apply
        $\mathrm{VS}_{p\approx1}$ or a score on $Y^2$ --- the only
        route by which the $\tau$-AR(2) structure becomes visible.
  \item \textbf{Confirm the attenuation:} compute the lag-1
        autocorrelation of the posterior-mean $\tau^\star$ series and
        divide by $\gamma_1=0.5$ to recover $a=q/(q+1)$; profile
        $\log L(\phi)$ on a grid to check for flatness near $0$.
  \item \textbf{Raise $\mathcal I(\phi)$:} more replicate series
        (larger $K$ or $T$), a non-centred parameterisation of
        $\tau^\star$ to break the Gibbs fixed point, a $\phi$-prior not
        concentrated at $0$, or marginalising the latent series so
        $\phi$ is informed by $L(\phi)$ directly.
  \item Run AR-order selection over $p\in\{0,1,2\}$ via DIC/WAIC so any
        observed advantage is not an artefact of a matched truth.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Reference R implementation}
\label{app:r-code}

The standardised AR(2) simulator in
\path{code/00_core/auxfunctions_ar2.R}.

\begin{lstlisting}
simulate_ar2_standard <- function(nt, n, phi1, phi2,
                                  tol = 1e-06, param_name = NULL) {
  # ... stationarity check ...
  denom  <- 1 - phi2
  gamma0 <- 1
  gamma1 <- phi1 * gamma0 / denom
  gamma2 <- phi1 * gamma1 + phi2 * gamma0
  sigma2 <- gamma0 - phi1 * gamma1 - phi2 * gamma2
  innov.sd <- sqrt(sigma2)

  ar2 <- matrix(0, nrow = n, ncol = nt)
  init.states <- draw_ar2_initial(n, gamma0, gamma1)
  ar2[, 1] <- init.states[, 1]
  ar2[, 2] <- init.states[, 2]

  for (t in 3:nt) {
    ar2[, t] <- phi1 * ar2[, t - 1] +
                phi2 * ar2[, t - 2] +
                rnorm(n, 0, innov.sd)
  }
  ar2
}
\end{lstlisting}

The construction matches Definition~\ref{def:standardised}: the
marginal variance is fixed at $\gamma_0 = 1$, the innovation variance
is derived, and initial states are drawn from the stationary bivariate
Gaussian via \texttt{draw\_ar2\_initial()}, removing the need for a
burn-in.

\section{Verification by reverse simulation}
\label{app:verification}

To confirm the marginal invariance empirically we simulate $n = 1000$
AR(2) paths under $(\phi_1, \phi_2) = (0.6, -0.3)$ via manual
recursion and via \texttt{stats::filter(\ldots, method =
"recursive")}, fit \texttt{arima(order = c(2, 0, 0))} to each, and run
a residual diagnostic. Both fits recover the true coefficients within
one standard error, and the Ljung--Box and Shapiro--Wilk tests on the
residuals are non-significant ($p > 0.4$). Manual recursion and
filtering produce statistically equivalent AR(2) series.

\section{Four-layer ACF attenuation diagnostic}
\label{app:acf-diagnostic}

To localise where the AR(2) signal is lost on the path from the latent
$z$ to the observable $Y$, decompose a method-8 posterior predictive
trajectory at a held-out site $s^*$ into four layers and compare their
empirical autocorrelations:
\begin{description}[leftmargin=2.6em, itemsep=2pt]
  \item[\textnormal{L1}] $z_{k^\ast,t}$ --- the source latent at a fixed
        knot; should match the theoretical AR(2) ACF implied by
        $\hat\phi_z$.
  \item[\textnormal{L2}] $z_{g(s^*,t),t}$ --- after membership
        reindexing; attenuation here measures knot-wandering loss.
  \item[\textnormal{L3}] $\mu_{s^*,t} = x_{s^*,t}^\top\beta +
        \lambda\,z_{g(s^*,t),t}$ --- after $\lambda$-gating.
  \item[\textnormal{L4}] $Y_t(s^*)$ --- the full predictive draw, after
        the i.i.d.-in-$t$ spatial noise; this is where most of the
        signal is expected to die.
\end{description}

\begin{lstlisting}
# Four-layer ACF attenuation diagnostic for one method-8 fit.
draws <- sample(seq_along(fit.1$lambda), 50)
k_star <- 1
for (d in draws) {
  beta_d   <- fit.1$beta[d, ]
  lambda_d <- fit.1$lambda[d]
  z_d      <- fit.1$z[, , d]
  knots_d  <- fit.1$knots[, , , d]

  L1 <- z_d[k_star, ]
  L2 <- sapply(seq_len(nt), function(t)
          z_d[mem(matrix(s.p[sp, ], 1, 2),
                  matrix(knots_d[, , t], nknots, 2)), t])
  L3 <- as.numeric(x.p[sp, , ] %*% beta_d) + lambda_d * L2
  L4 <- L3 + rnorm(nt, 0, sigma_g_t)        # iid spatial noise

  store(acf(L1, plot = FALSE)$acf[2:4],
        acf(L2, plot = FALSE)$acf[2:4],
        acf(L3, plot = FALSE)$acf[2:4],
        acf(L4, plot = FALSE)$acf[2:4])
}
# Average across draws; compare L1 to ARMAacf(ar = colMeans(fit.1$phi.z)).
\end{lstlisting}

If L1 matches the theoretical ACF but L4 is essentially white, the
signal is lost to the $\lambda$-gating and the spatial noise --- in
agreement with Proposition~\ref{prop:level-autocov} --- and no
level-based score can recover it. Use posterior \emph{parameter} draws
but the stored latent fields $z,\tau,w$ within each draw; resampling
the latents from their AR(2) prior would report the prior ACF rather
than the fitted one.
