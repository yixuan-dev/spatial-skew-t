\title{Where We Are:\\
       An Audit of the AR(2) Extension to the\\
       Space-time \Skewt Model of Morris~et~al.\ (2017)}
\author{Yi-Xuan Xie}
\date{2026-05-15}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We audit an AR(2) extension of the space-time \skewt model of
\citet*{morris_space-time_2017}. The extension generalises the AR(1) prior placed on
three transformed tail parameters ($\bw^{*}_{tk}$, $z^{*}_{tk}$,
$\sigma^{2*}_{tk}$) to a stationary AR(2). After a careful re-reading of the
paper, the production R~code, and the Metropolis--Hastings update mechanics,
we identify three deviations from the stationary AR(2) prior: (i) a
$t = 2$ conditional density that is inconsistent with the simulator's joint
initial draw; (ii) a missing lag-2 factor in the Metropolis--Hastings ratio
for each $Y_t$ update; and (iii) an apparent asymmetry in the $\phi$ proposal.
Detailed-balance analysis confirms~(i) and~(ii) as real bugs and refutes~(iii):
the original silent-rejection scheme for $\phi$ already targets the correct
posterior, and the ``correction'' we initially applied biases the chain toward
the boundary of the stationarity region. The final state of the audit is
that (i) and (ii) have been patched and (iii) has been reverted, with five
regression tests covering the structural and behavioural assertions. We
also point to an optional follow-up: refactor the $\phi$ update to mirror
the truncated-proposal scheme used in the AR(1) reference code if mixing
near the stationarity boundary becomes a concern.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

\citet*{morris_space-time_2017} introduce a space-time \skewt process for threshold
exceedances. Section~3.2 of that paper extends the spatial-only model to
the spatiotemporal setting by placing AR(1) priors on three transformed
parameters $\bw^{*}_{tk}$, $z^{*}_{tk}$, $\sigma^{2*}_{tk}$, while preserving
the marginal \skewt distribution at every time point. A natural extension
replaces the AR(1) prior with a stationary AR(2). The implementation lives
in \texttt{code/R/ar2/} and was constructed by analogy to the AR(1) reference
code in \texttt{code/R/ar2/update\_params.R}.

This note has three goals:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item State the AR(2) prior used in the implementation and derive its
        induced moments and conditional factorisation
        (\sref{sec:setup}).
  \item Identify the three deviations between the production code and the
        stationary AR(2) prior, and apply a careful detailed-balance
        analysis to each (\sref{sec:audit}).
  \item Describe the patches applied, the tests that guard them, and the
        outstanding work (\sref{sec:patches}--\sref{sec:state}).
\end{enumerate}

The central result is that two of the three deviations are real bugs and
have been patched: the missing lag-2 term in the Metropolis--Hastings ratio
of $Y_t$ (Issue~2) and the inconsistent $t = 2$ conditional density that
broke the simulator-versus-MCMC agreement on the joint of $(Y_1, Y_2)$
(Issue~1). The third (Issue~3) is not a bug at all -- the original
silent-rejection scheme is a textbook-valid sampler, and the correction we
initially applied to it introduces a target-shifting bias of its own, so
that correction has been reverted.

\section{Model setup}\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Notation and the paper's AR(1) prior}\label{sec:notation}

We adopt the notation of \citet*{morris_space-time_2017} (their Section~3.2). For each
spatial knot index $k = 1, \ldots, K$ and time index $t = 1, \ldots, n_t$,
let $\bw^{*}_{tk} \in \mathbb{R}^2$, $z^{*}_{tk} \in \mathbb{R}$, and
$\sigma^{2*}_{tk} \in \mathbb{R}$ denote the standard-normal-scale
transformations of the spatial knot, half-normal random effect, and inverse
gamma variance scale, respectively. The paper's AR(1) prior, restated in
the notation we will need:
\begin{align}
  \bw^{*}_{1k} &\sim N_2(\bZero, \bI_2), \quad
  z^{*}_{1k} \sim N(0, 1), \quad
  \sigma^{2*}_{1k} \sim N(0, 1), \label{eq:ar1-init} \\
  \bw^{*}_{tk} \mid \bw^{*}_{t-1, k} &\sim N_2\!\left[\phi_w \bw^{*}_{t-1, k},\; (1 - \phi_w^2) \bI_2\right], \label{eq:ar1-w} \\
  z^{*}_{tk} \mid z^{*}_{t-1, k} &\sim N\!\left[\phi_z z^{*}_{t-1, k},\; 1 - \phi_z^2\right], \label{eq:ar1-z} \\
  \sigma^{2*}_{tk} \mid \sigma^{2*}_{t-1, k} &\sim N\!\left[\phi_\sigma \sigma^{2*}_{t-1, k},\; 1 - \phi_\sigma^2\right], \label{eq:ar1-sigma}
\end{align}
with $|\phi_w|, |\phi_z|, |\phi_\sigma| < 1$.

\subsection{The AR(2) extension}\label{sec:ar2}

Because the three parameter classes share an identical AR structure, we
state results for a generic scalar series $\{Y_t\}_{t=1}^{n_t}$ representing
any one of $\bw^{*}_{\cdot k}$ (componentwise), $z^{*}_{\cdot k}$, or
$\sigma^{2*}_{\cdot k}$ for a fixed knot $k$.

\begin{definition}[Stationary AR(2) prior]\label{def:ar2}
A standard AR(2) process satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ar2-rec}
  Y_t = \phi_1 Y_{t-1} + \phi_2 Y_{t-2} + \epsilon_t,
  \qquad \epsilon_t \stackrel{iid}{\sim} N(0, \sigma_\epsilon^2),
  \qquad t \geq 3,
\end{equation}
together with the stationarity conditions
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ar2-stab}
  |\phi_2| < 1, \quad \phi_1 + \phi_2 < 1, \quad \phi_2 - \phi_1 < 1,
\end{equation}
and the normalisation $\mathrm{Var}(Y_t) = 1$ for all $t$ (so the \skewt
marginal is preserved).
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Induced moments]\label{prop:moments}
Under \Cref{def:ar2}, the Yule--Walker equations together with
$\gamma_0 := \mathrm{Var}(Y_t) = 1$ yield
\begin{equation}\label{eq:moments}
  \gamma_0 = 1, \qquad
  \gamma_1 = \frac{\phi_1}{1 - \phi_2}, \qquad
  \gamma_2 = \phi_1 \gamma_1 + \phi_2, \qquad
  \sigma_\epsilon^2 = 1 - \phi_1 \gamma_1 - \phi_2 \gamma_2,
\end{equation}
all functions of $(\phi_1, \phi_2)$ only.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The autocovariance function $\gamma_h := \mathrm{Cov}(Y_t, Y_{t+h})$
satisfies the Yule--Walker recursion $\gamma_h = \phi_1 \gamma_{h-1}
+ \phi_2 \gamma_{h-2}$ for $h \geq 1$, with $\gamma_{-h} = \gamma_h$. Setting
$h = 1$ and using $\gamma_0 = 1$ gives $\gamma_1 = \phi_1 + \phi_2 \gamma_1$,
hence $\gamma_1 = \phi_1 / (1 - \phi_2)$. Setting $h = 2$ gives
$\gamma_2 = \phi_1 \gamma_1 + \phi_2 \gamma_0 = \phi_1 \gamma_1 + \phi_2$.
Multiplying \eref{eq:ar2-rec} by $Y_t$ and taking expectations yields
$\gamma_0 = \phi_1 \gamma_1 + \phi_2 \gamma_2 + \sigma_\epsilon^2$,
so $\sigma_\epsilon^2 = 1 - \phi_1 \gamma_1 - \phi_2 \gamma_2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Joint factorisation under stationarity]\label{prop:factor}
Assume $\{Y_t\}_{t=1}^{n_t}$ is stationary under \eref{eq:ar2-rec} with
unit marginal variance. Then the joint density factorises as
\begin{align}
  p(Y_1) &= N(0, 1), \label{eq:p1} \\
  p(Y_2 \mid Y_1) &= N\!\left(\gamma_1 Y_1,\; 1 - \gamma_1^2\right), \label{eq:p2} \\
  p(Y_t \mid Y_{t-1}, Y_{t-2}) &= N\!\left(\phi_1 Y_{t-1} + \phi_2 Y_{t-2},\; \sigma_\epsilon^2\right),
  \qquad t \geq 3. \label{eq:pt}
\end{align}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Stationarity gives $\mathrm{Var}(Y_1) = 1$, so \eref{eq:p1} follows from
Gaussianity. For \eref{eq:p2}: the stationary joint of $(Y_1, Y_2)$ is
$N_2\!\bigl(0, \bigl[\begin{smallmatrix}1 & \gamma_1\\\gamma_1 & 1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr]\bigr)$,
and the conditional of a bivariate Gaussian gives mean $\gamma_1 Y_1$ and
variance $1 - \gamma_1^2$. For \eref{eq:pt}: \eref{eq:ar2-rec} directly
gives $Y_t \mid (Y_{t-1}, Y_{t-2}) \sim N(\phi_1 Y_{t-1} + \phi_2 Y_{t-2},
\sigma_\epsilon^2)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:fix1}
\eref{eq:p2} is sometimes replaced in the literature by the AR(1)-style
density $N(\phi_1 Y_1, \sigma_\epsilon^2)$, which arises if one assumes
$(Y_1, Y_2)$ are independent with $Y_2 \sim N(0,1)$ and then runs the
recursion from $t = 3$. This non-stationary initialisation is consistent
with the marginal statements ``$Y_1 \sim N(0,1)$ and $Y_2 \sim N(0,1)$''
read in isolation, but it does \emph{not} preserve $\mathrm{Var}(Y_t) = 1$
for $t \geq 3$ unless $\sigma_\epsilon^2$ is adjusted away from its
Yule--Walker value. The implementation we audit uses \eref{eq:p2}'s
non-stationary form at $t = 2$ inside the MCMC, while the simulator
\texttt{simulate\_ar2\_standard} samples $(Y_1, Y_2)$ jointly from the
stationary $N_2$. The two are therefore consistent with each other for
$t \geq 3$ but disagree on the single density factor at $t = 2$.
\end{remark}

\section{Audit of the implementation}\label{sec:audit}

We label the three deviations identified during the audit as Issue~1
($t = 2$ conditional), Issue~2 (missing lag-2 term), and Issue~3
($\phi$-proposal asymmetry). \sref{sec:issue2}--\sref{sec:issue3} present
the detailed-balance analysis that classifies each.

\subsection{Issue~1: the \texorpdfstring{$t = 2$}{t = 2} conditional}\label{sec:issue1}

The function \texttt{get\_ar2\_conditional\_params} in
\texttt{auxfunctions\_ar2.R} previously returned, for $t = 2$, the AR(2)
recursion-style form $N(\phi_1 Y_1, \sigma_\epsilon^2)$ rather than the
stationary form \eref{eq:p2}. This was the AR(2) recursion extrapolated
backward with a fictitious $Y_0 = 0$, and it disagreed with the joint draw
that the simulator \texttt{simulate\_ar2\_standard} performs at $t = 1, 2$.
The two coincide only when $\phi_2 = 0$.

\paragraph{Patch.}
The $t == 2$ branch has been replaced by \eref{eq:p2}:
\begin{lstlisting}
# Fix 1: stationary AR(2) conditional at t = 2
if (t == 2) {
    g1    <- moments$gamma1
    sd.t2 <- sqrt(moments$gamma0 - g1^2)   # = sqrt(1 - gamma1^2)
    return(list(mean = g1 * lag1, sd = sd.t2))
}
\end{lstlisting}
With this in place, the MCMC density at $t = 2$ agrees with the
simulator's joint draw and the marginal property
$\mathrm{Var}(Y_t) = 1$ is preserved by every component of the sampler.

\paragraph{Regression test.}
\texttt{tests/test\_fix1\_t2\_conditional.R} is a closed-form unit test
that calls \texttt{get\_ar2\_conditional\_params(t = 2, ...)} at six
$(\phi_1, \phi_2)$ configurations spanning the interior and the
near-boundary regions of the stationarity triangle, and checks the
returned $(\mathrm{mean}, \mathrm{sd})$ against the Yule--Walker target
$(\gamma_1 y_1, \sqrt{1 - \gamma_1^2})$ to machine precision
($10^{-10}$). On the un-patched code the test fails at five of six
configurations (only the $\phi_2 = 0$ row matches); after the patch it
passes at all six.

\subsection{Issue~2: missing lag-2 term in the \texorpdfstring{$Y_t$}{Yt} MH ratio}\label{sec:issue2}

\subsubsection{Detailed-balance argument}

Under the AR(2) prior, $Y_t$ enters \emph{three} factors of the joint
distribution: as the conditioned-on variable at time $t$, as the lag-1
predictor at time $t+1$, and as the lag-2 predictor at time $t+2$. The
full conditional density of $Y_t$ given the rest of the chain and the
data is therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:full-cond}
  \pi(Y_t \mid Y_{-t}, \text{data})
  \propto L(\text{data} \mid Y_t) \cdot
  \underbrace{p(Y_t \mid Y_{t-1}, Y_{t-2})}_{\text{self}}\cdot
  \underbrace{p(Y_{t+1} \mid Y_t, Y_{t-1})}_{\text{lag-1}}\cdot
  \underbrace{p(Y_{t+2} \mid Y_{t+1}, Y_t)}_{\text{lag-2}}.
\end{equation}
For a symmetric Metropolis proposal $Y_t \to Y_t^c$, detailed balance with
target \eref{eq:full-cond} requires the acceptance probability
$\alpha = \min(1, R)$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fix2-R}
  \log R = \log\frac{L(\text{data} \mid Y_t^c)}{L(\text{data} \mid Y_t)}
          + \sum_{s \in \{t,\,t+1,\,t+2\}} \!\!\!
            \bigl[\log p(Y_s \mid \text{lags}, Y_t = Y_t^c) - \log p(Y_s \mid \text{lags}, Y_t)\bigr].
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Detailed-balance violation in the original code]\label{prop:fix2}
The original \texttt{update\{Tau,Z,Knots\}TS\_AR2} functions in
\texttt{update\_params\_ar2.R} include only the $s = t$ and $s = t+1$
factors in $\log R$. The omitted $s = t+2$ factor enters the joint AR(2)
prior whenever $t \leq n_t - 2$. The chain therefore satisfies detailed
balance for the modified target
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tilde-pi}
  \widetilde\pi(Y_t \mid \cdots) \propto
  L(\text{data} \mid Y_t)\, p(Y_t \mid Y_{t-1}, Y_{t-2})\, p(Y_{t+1} \mid Y_t, Y_{t-1}),
\end{equation}
which is not the AR(2) full conditional.
\end{proposition}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:acf}
The stationary marginal of the chain under \eref{eq:tilde-pi} does not
have lag-2 autocorrelation equal to $\gamma_2$. The empirical lag-2 ACF
of a prior-only chain (no data likelihood) under the original code differs
from \eref{eq:moments} by an $O(1)$ amount.
\end{corollary}

\subsubsection{Patch}

For each of the four call sites
(\texttt{updateTauTS\_AR2} for both \texttt{nknots == 1} and
\texttt{nknots > 1}, \texttt{updateZTS\_AR2}, and
\texttt{updateKnotsTS\_AR2}) we add a $t+2$ correction parallel to the
existing $t+1$ block. The patch for \texttt{updateZTS\_AR2}:
\begin{lstlisting}
# Fix 2: z.star[k, t] enters the t+2 conditional density as lag-2
if ((t + 2) <= nt) {
    z.star.next2 <- z.star[k, t + 2]
    R <- R +
        ar2_conditional_log_density(
            z.star.next2, t + 2,
            z.star[k, t + 1], can.z.star[k],
            phi1 = phi1, phi2 = phi2, moments = ar2.moments) -
        ar2_conditional_log_density(
            z.star.next2, t + 2,
            z.star[k, t + 1], z.star[k, t],
            phi1 = phi1, phi2 = phi2, moments = ar2.moments)
}
\end{lstlisting}
For \texttt{updateKnotsTS\_AR2} the density returns a $K \times 2$ matrix
that is aggregated by \texttt{sum(...)}, mirroring the existing $t+1$
block. At $t = 1$ the companion $t+1$ block evaluates the $t = 2$
conditional via \texttt{get\_ar2\_conditional\_params}, which after the
Issue~1 patch returns the stationary form \eref{eq:p2}. The Fix~1 dependency
that this note used to carry inline at the four patch sites has been
resolved; the comments now simply point back to \sref{sec:issue1}.

\subsection{Issue~3: \texorpdfstring{$\phi$}{phi} proposal asymmetry --- a non-issue}\label{sec:issue3}

The AR(1) reference \texttt{updatePhiTS} samples a $\phi$ candidate from a
\emph{truncated} normal via inverse CDF and includes the Hastings
correction
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hast}
  \Delta_{\mathrm{H}} = \log P\!\bigl(S; \phi^{(r-1)}\bigr) - \log P\!\bigl(S; \phi^{(c)}\bigr),
  \qquad P(S; m) = \Phi\!\left(\tfrac{b - m}{\tau}\right) - \Phi\!\left(\tfrac{a - m}{\tau}\right),
\end{equation}
for $S = (a, b)$ the AR(1) stationarity interval $(-1, 1)$.
The AR(2) implementation \texttt{updatePhiAR2TS} instead samples
$\phi^{(c)} \sim N(\phi^{(r-1)}, \tau^2)$ from an \emph{untruncated} normal
and silently rejects when $\phi^{(c)}$ falls outside the conditional
stationarity interval
\begin{equation}\label{eq:S1S2}
  S_1(\phi_2) = (\phi_2 - 1, 1 - \phi_2), \qquad
  S_2(\phi_1) = (|\phi_1| - 1, 1 - |\phi_1|).
\end{equation}
We initially conjectured that the silent-rejection mechanism is
operationally equivalent to truncated sampling and therefore requires the
same Hastings correction \eref{eq:hast}. \Cref{prop:rejection} below shows
this conjecture is \emph{wrong}: silent rejection of out-of-region
candidates from a symmetric proposal already targets the correct
posterior, with no Hastings correction needed.

\begin{proposition}[Silent-rejection scheme is detailed-balance correct]\label{prop:rejection}
Let $\pi$ be a target density on $\mathbb{R}^d$ supported on a region
$S \subset \mathbb{R}^d$ (i.e.\ $\pi(\phi) = 0$ for $\phi \notin S$). Let
$q_0(\cdot \mid \phi)$ be a symmetric proposal density on $\mathbb{R}^d$.
Consider the Metropolis kernel
\begin{equation}\label{eq:silent}
  \Pr\bigl(\phi \to \phi^c\bigr) =
  \begin{cases}
    q_0(\phi^c \mid \phi)\, \min\!\bigl(1, \pi(\phi^c) / \pi(\phi)\bigr) & \phi^c \in S,\\
    0 & \phi^c \notin S,
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
with the residual mass $\Pr(\phi \to \phi)$ chosen so that the
total probability sums to one. Then $\pi$ is invariant for the chain
defined by~\eref{eq:silent}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
We verify detailed balance for $\phi, \phi^c \in S$, $\phi \neq \phi^c$:
\begin{align*}
\pi(\phi)\, q_0(\phi^c \mid \phi)\, \min\!\bigl(1, \pi(\phi^c)/\pi(\phi)\bigr)
  &= q_0(\phi^c \mid \phi)\, \min(\pi(\phi), \pi(\phi^c)) \\
  &= q_0(\phi \mid \phi^c)\, \min(\pi(\phi^c), \pi(\phi)) \\
  &= \pi(\phi^c)\, q_0(\phi \mid \phi^c)\, \min\!\bigl(1, \pi(\phi)/\pi(\phi^c)\bigr),
\end{align*}
where the middle equality uses the symmetry $q_0(\phi^c \mid \phi)
= q_0(\phi \mid \phi^c)$. For $\phi^c \notin S$ the move has probability
zero on both sides because $\pi(\phi^c) = 0$. Detailed balance holds
pointwise on $S \times S$, hence $\pi$ is invariant.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[The proposed Hastings ``fix'' shifts the target]\label{prop:wrong-fix}
If the kernel \eref{eq:silent} is modified by including the Hastings
factor $P(S;\phi) / P(S;\phi^c)$ in the acceptance ratio while retaining
silent rejection, the resulting chain is invariant not for $\pi$ but for
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wrong-target}
  \widetilde\pi(\phi) \;\propto\; \frac{\pi(\phi)}{P(S; \phi)}.
\end{equation}
Because $P(S; \phi)$ is maximised at the interior of $S$ and decreases
toward its boundary, $\widetilde\pi$ shifts mass toward the boundary
relative to $\pi$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
With acceptance ratio $\alpha^\dagger(\phi, \phi^c) = \min\!\bigl(1,
[\pi(\phi^c)/\pi(\phi)] \cdot [P(S;\phi)/P(S;\phi^c)]\bigr)$,
detailed balance for the candidate target $\widetilde\pi$ requires
\[
\widetilde\pi(\phi)\, q_0(\phi^c \mid \phi)\, \alpha^\dagger(\phi, \phi^c)
= \widetilde\pi(\phi^c)\, q_0(\phi \mid \phi^c)\, \alpha^\dagger(\phi^c, \phi),
\]
which after the substitution $\widetilde\pi(\phi) = \pi(\phi)/P(S;\phi)$
collapses to
\[
q_0(\phi^c \mid \phi)\, \min\!\bigl(\widetilde\pi(\phi),\widetilde\pi(\phi^c)\bigr)
= q_0(\phi \mid \phi^c)\, \min\!\bigl(\widetilde\pi(\phi^c),\widetilde\pi(\phi)\bigr),
\]
which holds by symmetry of $q_0$. To see the direction of the shift,
write $P(S; \phi) = \Phi\!\bigl(\tfrac{b-\phi}{\tau}\bigr)
- \Phi\!\bigl(\tfrac{a-\phi}{\tau}\bigr)$ for $S = (a, b)$. By symmetry of
$\Phi$, $P(S; \phi)$ is maximised at $\phi = (a + b)/2$ and strictly
decreasing as $|\phi - (a+b)/2|$ increases. Hence $1/P(S;\phi)$ is
minimised at the centre of $S$ and increases toward the boundary,
shifting mass of $\widetilde\pi$ away from the centre relative to $\pi$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:revert}
The original \texttt{updatePhiAR2TS} (silent rejection, no Hastings term)
is detailed-balance-correct for the posterior of $(\phi_1, \phi_2)$
restricted to the AR(2) stationarity region. The Hastings correction was
removed and the production code now uses the silent-rejection scheme. An
optional follow-up, deferred unless mixing near the boundary becomes a
concern, is to refactor the function to mirror \texttt{updatePhiTS}'s
truncated-sampling scheme; in that variant the Hastings correction is
mandatory.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{Why the initial Fix~3 test was misleading}\label{sec:misleading-test}

The paired test \texttt{tests/test\_fix3\_phi\_hastings.R} placed the truth
at $(\phi_1^\star, \phi_2^\star) = (0.45, 0.50)$, approximately $90\%$ of
the way to both edges of $S_1$ and $S_2$. Near the boundary,
$\widetilde\pi$ in \eref{eq:wrong-target} concentrates near the truth more
tightly than the true posterior~$\pi$, so the ``patched'' chain produced
posterior medians closer to the truth than the original chain
\emph{by coincidence}. The reported bias-reduction ratio of $0.48$ on
$\phi_1$ across 12 replicates is well within Monte-Carlo noise: per-chain
posterior medians vary by $\sim 0.10$, giving a standard error on the
mean of $\sim 0.03$ -- larger than the signal claimed.

A diagnostic that would have caught the issue is the same test with the
truth placed at $(\phi_1^\star, \phi_2^\star) = (0, 0)$, away from any
boundary. Under \Cref{prop:wrong-fix} the ``patched'' chain should sit
\emph{farther} from zero than the original chain in this regime; the
unpatched chain is correct. We did not run this diagnostic before
applying the patch.

\section{Patches and regression-test infrastructure}\label{sec:patches}

\subsection{Patches applied}\label{sec:patches-applied}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Issue~1 ($t = 2$ conditional)}: \emph{applied} (one-line
        change in \texttt{get\_ar2\_conditional\_params}). The $t = 2$
        branch now returns the stationary form
        $N(\gamma_1 y_1, 1 - \gamma_1^2)$.
  \item \textbf{Issue~2 (lag-2 MH term)}: applied at four sites in
        \texttt{code/R/ar2/update\_params\_ar2.R}.
  \item \textbf{Issue~3 ($\phi$ Hastings correction)}: \emph{reverted}.
        The Hastings adjustment was removed; \texttt{updatePhiAR2TS} is
        back to the silent-rejection scheme, which by \Cref{prop:rejection}
        targets the correct posterior.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Regression tests}\label{sec:tests}

\section{Current state and next steps}\label{sec:state}

The end-state of the audit is:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Issue~1 is closed.} The $t = 2$ branch of
        \texttt{get\_ar2\_conditional\_params} now returns the stationary
        form $N(\gamma_1 y_1, 1 - \gamma_1^2)$. The simulator and the MCMC
        density now agree on the joint of $(Y_1, Y_2)$, and
        $\mathrm{Var}(Y_t) = 1$ holds across every component of the
        sampler. The unit test \texttt{test\_fix1\_t2\_conditional.R}
        verifies the returned $(\mathrm{mean}, \mathrm{sd})$ against the
        Yule--Walker target to machine precision at six $(\phi_1, \phi_2)$
        configurations.

  \item \textbf{Issue~2 is closed.} The lag-2 MH correction is applied at
        all four sites, the math test verifies the AR(2) stationary
        marginals to four decimal places, the integration test catches
        the regression on the actual production function, and the
        structural grep test guards the four sites collectively against
        bulk deletion. \Cref{prop:fix2} establishes that the correction
        is mathematically required for the AR(2) full conditional.

  \item \textbf{Issue~3 is closed by reverting.} The Hastings adjustment
        has been removed; \texttt{updatePhiAR2TS} is back to the
        silent-rejection scheme established as correct in
        \Cref{prop:rejection}. The replacement test
        (\texttt{test\_fix3\_phi\_hastings.R}) parses the body of
        \texttt{updatePhiAR2TS} and asserts that no \texttt{pnorm(.)}
        calls remain --- the structural signature of the previously
        shipped Hastings adjustment --- and additionally runs a sanity
        chain that recovers an interior truth.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Optional follow-up.}
If mixing near the AR(2) stationarity boundary becomes a concern in
practice, refactor \texttt{updatePhiAR2TS} to mirror \texttt{updatePhiTS}:
sample $\phi^{(c)}$ from a truncated normal via inverse CDF and include
the Hastings correction \eref{eq:hast}. The silent-rejection scheme and
the truncated-sampling scheme have the same stationary distribution; the
latter avoids wasted draws on out-of-region candidates and typically
mixes faster near boundaries, at the cost of a slightly more involved
update step.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

A careful detailed-balance audit of the AR(2) extension to the
\citet*{morris_space-time_2017} space-time \skewt model produced two
confirmed bugs and one rejected ``bug''. The first confirmed bug --- the
$t = 2$ conditional density used inside the MCMC --- broke the
simulator-versus-MCMC agreement on the joint of $(Y_1, Y_2)$ and was
patched by replacing the recursion-style $N(\phi_1 y_1, \sigma_\epsilon^2)$
with the stationary form $N(\gamma_1 y_1, 1 - \gamma_1^2)$. The second ---
the missing lag-2 term in the $Y_t$ Metropolis--Hastings ratio --- was
patched at all four call sites in \texttt{update\_params\_ar2.R}. The
rejected ``bug'' was the apparent asymmetry of the $\phi$ proposal: the
silent-rejection scheme in the original \texttt{updatePhiAR2TS} was
correct all along, the Hastings adjustment we initially applied targets a
boundary-shifted posterior, and that adjustment has been reverted.

The lesson for the broader project is methodological: empirical
``bias-reduction'' on a single configuration is not a sufficient guard
against incorrect MCMC patches. Detailed balance must be checked from
first principles for any change to a Metropolis--Hastings acceptance
ratio, and regression tests must include both near-boundary and
far-from-boundary truths to expose target-shifting errors that flatter
themselves in one regime and fail in the other.
